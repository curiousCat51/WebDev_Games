Cubes

Cubes are interactive objects in the Portal series that can be moved, stacked, and used to solve puzzles. They are typically made of a durable material and can be pushed, pulled, and placed in various configurations to create platforms or activate switches.

Types of Cubes

Weighted Storage Cube

The Weighted Storage Cube was first introduced in Portal, and remains prevalent in Portal 2.
In Portal 2 however, the cube is given a blue ring in the center instead of a blue cross, and can turn yellow when placed upon a Super Button.

Companion Cube

The Companion Cube is a special type of Weighted Storage Cube that is introduced in Portal.
It is distinguished by its heart-shaped markings and is often used in puzzles that require the player to carry it through test chambers. The Companion Cube has become an iconic symbol of the Portal series, representing companionship and emotional attachment.

Discouragement Redirection Cube

The Discouragement Redirection Cube was introduced in Portal 2. It's main purpose is to redirect Thermal Discouragement Beams.
It can also act as a Weighted Storage Cube to activate Heavy-Duty Super Buttons.

Edgeless Safety Cube

The Edgeless Safety Cube is a type of cube that was introduced in Portal 2.
It is characterized by its smooth, edgeless design and is often used in puzzles that require precise placement. The cube's unique shape makes it useful for creating stable platforms and solving complex mechanical challenges. It appears in various test chambers during the co-op campaign.

Frankenturret

The Frankenturret is a type of cube that was introduced in Portal 2.
They consist of a combination of two turret heads and legs fused with a Weighted Storage Cube. Wheatley created them as a replacement for the player. Instead of solving the chambers themselves they will aimlessly hop around while not placed on a button. If fallen over they start waving their legs trying to get up.

Contraption Cube

The Contraption Cube is exclusively featured in the Portal 2 - Educational Version Puzzle Creator.
It has adjustable mass, friction and elasticity. The minimum mass is 1kg, maximum mass is 85kg. There are 5 levels for friction: frictionless, slippery, normal, sticky and very sticky.
There are also 5 levels for elasticity: no rebound, slight rebound, normal, bouncy, very bouncy. The default settings for the Contraption Cube are 40kg, frictionless, and no rebound. Different settings cause the cube to take on slightly different appearances. It has 2,125 possible variations. It has its own button, the Contraption Cube Receptacle.

Schrödinger Cube

The Schrödinger cubes are featured in the Portal 2: Community Edition and Portal: Revolution. These cubes behave like Discouragement Redirection Cubes.
When a laser enters either cube, it always exits from the other cube. It doesn't matter which cube the laser enters; it will always come out from the opposite one.

Skull Cube

The skull cube is an alternate design for the regular Weighted Storage Cube in which the Aperture logo is replaced by a crudely drawn skull.
It is present in the game files of Portal: Still Alive, but does not appear in-game.
Due to a similar design existing for pedestal buttons that are connected to cube droppers, is is widely believed that said cube droppers would have originally dispensed skull cubes, but that this was cut due to players being confused into thinking the cubes had a unique function. The unofficial PC port of Still Alive reimplements the skull cubes, but they are not used in any official version of the game.
